        The comeback

        Simone Biles has been doing gymnastics since she was six years old, and she has collected more medals than any other gymnast in history. She has won eleven Olympic medals, seven of them gold, and several skills in the sport have been named after her.

        Yet her most famous Olympic moment was not a victory. When she arrived in Tokyo in 2021, she had been training for the Games for five years and was expected to win almost everything. But during the team final something frightening happened: in the air, she suddenly lost track of where she was. Gymnasts call this “the twisties”. In a sport where one wrong landing can cause a serious injury, Biles decided to withdraw from several finals. Some people criticised her, but many others praised her for putting her mental health first. She still went home with two medals, a team silver and a bronze on the beam.

        For a long time, nobody knew if she would compete again. She had been working quietly with a therapist when, in 2023, she returned and won the world all-around title once more. At the Paris 2024 Olympics she won gold with the team, in the all-around and on vault, and silver on the floor behind Brazil's Rebeca Andrade. On the podium, Biles and her teammate Jordan Chiles bowed down to Andrade, a gesture of respect that went around the world.

        Since Paris, Biles has been thinking about the 2028 Games in Los Angeles. She will be 31 by then, and she and her husband, NFL player Jonathan Owens, would like to start a family. “I don't necessarily have to make it just yet,” she has said about her decision.

          The four perfects

          The main grammar of the lesson. Present Perfect and Present Perfect Continuous connect the past with now; Past Perfect and Past Perfect Continuous connect it with a moment in the past.

          20 min

          Present Perfect

          have / has + V3

          Experience, result or a finished action whose time isn't important. How many / how much.

          Biles has won eleven Olympic medals.

          ever, never, already, yet, just, so far, three times

          Present Perfect Continuous

          have / has been + -ing

          An activity that started in the past and is still going on, or has just stopped with a visible result. How long.

          She has been doing gymnastics since 2003.

          for, since, how long, all day, lately, recently

          Past Perfect

          had + V3

          An action completed before another moment in the past.

          By the time she arrived in Paris, she had already won seven Olympic medals.

          before, after, by the time, already, when

          Past Perfect Continuous

          had been + -ing

          An activity in progress up to a moment in the past; often explains a past situation.

          When she arrived in Tokyo, she had been training for five years.

          for, since, how long, all day + a past moment
          Where is the reference point?

          Пастки: з точним часом у минулому (in 2016, last year, ago) вживаємо Past Simple, а не Present Perfect. Дієслова стану (know, like, believe, have = мати) у Continuous не вживаються: She has known her coach for years.
