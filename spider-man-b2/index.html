Choices &Consequences

A lesson about secrets, second chances and one very bad spell. Students talk about responsibility, learn twelve high-level words from the film, analyse the trailer and master third and mixed conditionals for talking about past regrets.

01

Warm-up 10 min

Get students talking before they see a single word list. Start with the quote, then play a round of “Would you rather”.

“With great power comes great responsibility.”
The most famous line of the Spider-Man stories
Discuss in pairs
  1. Do you agree with the quote? Who in real life has “great power”: politicians, influencers, parents, teachers?
  2. Have you ever kept a big secret from people close to you? Was it the right decision?
  3. Why do you think superhero films are so popular with adults, not only with kids?
  4. If you could go back and change one decision in your life, would you? Why or why not?
Would you rather…?

Pick a side. Defend it.

    04

    Video work 20 min

    Watch the official teaser trailer (about 3 minutes). Do the tasks before, while and after watching.

    Watch the teaser trailer Opens YouTube · Sony Pictures · Aug 2021
    Before watching · predict
    1. At the end of the previous film, the whole world learns that Peter Parker is Spider-Man. How would your life change if your biggest secret went viral?
    2. Peter asks a powerful wizard for help. What do you think he asks for?
    3. The title is No Way Home. What could it mean? Think of at least two meanings.
    While watching · true or false?
      Second viewing · put events in order

      Tap the events in the order they happen in the story.

        After watching · discuss
        1. Was Peter’s request to Doctor Strange selfish or understandable? Use words from the cards.
        2. Why does the spell backfire? Whose fault is it: Peter’s, Strange’s, or both?
        3. Is it ever right to wipe someone’s memory to protect them? Think of real-life equivalents: hiding bad news, white lies.
        4. If you were Peter, would you have accepted that people know your secret identity?

        Teacher tip: question 4 is a natural lead-in to the grammar. Write students’ answers on the board and come back to them in stage 05.

        05

        Grammar: past regrets 20 min

        Third conditional, mixed conditionals and wish / if only. The whole plot of the film is one long chain of “what ifs”.

        Third conditional past → past
        If + had + V3, would have + V3

        If Mysterio hadn’t revealed his name, Peter wouldn’t have gone to Doctor Strange.

        Mixed conditional 1 past cause → present result
        If + had + V3, would + V1

        If the spell hadn’t gone wrong, the villains wouldn’t be in New York now.

        Mixed conditional 2 present trait → past result
        If + past simple, would have + V3

        If Peter weren’t so kind-hearted, he would have sent the villains straight back.

        Wish / If only regret about the past
        wish / if only + had + V3

        Peter wishes he hadn’t interrupted the spell.

        Timeline

        Where is the cause? Where is the result?

        Watch out
        • No would in the if-clause: If he would have listened… → If he had listened…
        • Were is preferred for all persons in formal English: If Peter were less stubborn…
        • Inversion for B2+ style: Had he listened to Strange, none of this would have happened.

          Exercise B · which sentence is correct?
          06

          Speak & write 5 min + homework

          Use the new grammar and vocabulary in free speaking, then take the topic home.

          The butterfly chain: “If Peter hadn’t been bitten by a spider, he wouldn’t have…” Each student continues the chain with the result of the previous sentence. Who can keep it going longest?

          Mini-debate: should Peter have saved the villains?

          Role 1
          The Idealist

          Everyone deserves a second chance. Peter was right to try to cure the villains, even though it cost him a lot.

          Role 2
          The Realist

          The villains were dangerous. If Peter had sent them home at once, no one would have been hurt. Responsibility means protecting people first.

          Role 3
          The Judge

          Listen to both sides, ask follow-up questions and announce a verdict. Explain your decision with at least two conditionals.

          HOMEWORK

          Dear Peter…

          Write a letter to Peter Parker (180–220 words). Tell him what you think about the choices he made in the film and give him advice for the future.
