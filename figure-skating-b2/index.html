    Falls, comebacks and a golden disco

    The Olympic figure skating competition was supposed to be Ilia Malinin's moment. The American, the only skater who has ever landed a quadruple Axel in competition, arrived in Milan as a two-time world champion and the clear favourite. Instead, the “Quad God” fell twice in the free skate and finished eighth. Afterwards he admitted that the nerves had been “so overwhelming”. “I blew it,” he told reporters.

    His mistakes opened the door for Mikhail Shaidorov. Skating to music by the Kazakh singer Dimash Kudaibergen, he landed five clean quadruple jumps, something he had never managed in competition, and won Kazakhstan's first Olympic gold in the sport. He dedicated the medal to his country and to Denis Ten, who had won Kazakhstan's first Olympic figure skating medal, a bronze, in 2014.

    The women's event produced an even bigger comeback story. Alysa Liu had retired in 2022, when she was only 16. She returned to the ice for the 2024–25 season and became world champion. In Milan she climbed from third after the short program to gold, skating to Donna Summer's “MacArthur Park” in a gold dress that had taken more than 100 hours to make. She was the first American woman to win individual Olympic gold since 2002. Kaori Sakamoto of Japan, in her final season, took silver, and 17-year-old Ami Nakai won bronze in her debut senior season.

    In pairs, Japan's Riku Miura and Ryuichi Kihara were only fifth after the short program, but their free skate set a record score of 158.13 and gave Japan its first Olympic pairs title.

    Not every story ended happily. France's Laurence Fournier Beaudry and Guillaume Cizeron won ice dance by less than 1.5 points despite errors in their twizzles. Then it emerged that the French judge had scored the American silver medallists, Madison Chock and Evan Bates, more than five points below the other judges' average. Many fans called it a judging controversy. The ISU insisted it had “full confidence in the scores”, and Chock and Bates chose not to appeal.

      SEG05

      Grammar: reported speech & reporting verbs

      Journalists rarely quote everything word for word. They report it. Here's how, with real quotes from Milan.

      1 · Backshift

      After a past reporting verb (said, told, admitted), the tense usually moves one step back.

      Direct speechReported speech
      “I want to change the sport.”He said he wanted to change the sport.
      “The nerves are overwhelming.”He said the nerves were overwhelming.
      “I blew it.”He admitted he had blown it.
      “I've never landed five quads.”He said he had never landed five quads.
      “I'll come back.”He promised he would come back.
      “I can do it.”She said she could do it.

      Також змінюються слова часу й місця: today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, yesterday → the day before, here → there, this → that.

      2 · Say or tell? Questions

      say (something) · tell + person: He told reporters he had blown it. Never he said me.

      In reported questions the word order is like a statement, with no do/does/did:

      “Will you skate next season?”They asked if she would skate the next season.
      “Why did you change the program?”They asked why he had changed the program.

      Backshift не обов'язковий, якщо факт досі правдивий: He said medals don't define a person. Обидва варіанти правильні.

      “Medals don't define a person.”Ilia Malinin, 2026
      3 · Reporting verbs: the B2 upgrade

      Instead of said every time, choose a verb that shows how or why someone spoke. Each verb has its own pattern.

      verb + to-infinitivepromise, refuse, agree, offer, threaten, decide
      He promised to come back stronger.
      verb + person + to-inf.advise, tell, ask, warn, encourage, remind
      The coach warned him not to look at the scores.
      verb + -ingadmit, deny, suggest, recommend
      She denied seeing the scores.
      verb + preposition + -ingapologise for, insist on, congratulate sb on, accuse sb of, blame sb for
      He congratulated Shaidorov on winning.
      verb + that-clauseadmit, claim, explain, insist, announce, complain
      The ISU insisted that the scores were fair.

            Elements guide

            How to read figure skating

            Що роблять фігуристи на льоду, як називаються елементи англійською та українською і як читати протокол суддів. Наприкінці є декодер кодів і міні-тест «Який це стрибок?».

            DisciplinesScoringJumpsJump quizMarksSpinsStepsPairsIce danceDecoder

            Disciplines & programs

            Four disciplines, each with two programs. At the Olympics there's also a team event.

            Men's / Women's singlesSP + FS

            Short program (≈ 2 min 40 s) with required elements, then the free skate (≈ 4 min).

            PairsSP + FS

            A man and a woman. Lifts, throws and twists that singles skaters never do.

            Ice danceRD + FD

            Rhythm dance and free dance. No big jumps: the focus is on edges, rhythm and partnering.

            Team eventOlympics

            Ten countries; skaters from all four disciplines earn points for their team.

            Scoring

            Every element has a fixed base value. Nine judges then give it a Grade of Execution (GOE) from −5 to +5. The program components measure the artistry.

            TESTechnical score: base values + GOE+ PCSComposition · Presentation · Skating skills− Deductionsfalls (−1 each, more for repeated falls), time, costume= Segment scoreSP + FS = total

            The six jumps

            From easiest to hardest. The skater takes off from one foot and lands backwards on the right foot (for most skaters). In toe jumps they push off with the toe pick of the other foot; edge jumps start from the blade's edge alone. The Axel is the only jump with a forward take-off, so it has an extra half rotation.

            Quiz · Which jump is it?

            Read the description and choose the jump.

              Marks on the protocol

              Small symbols after a jump code tell you what went wrong. They lower the score.

              MarkNameWhat it means
              qQuarterThe jump is a quarter of a rotation short. Small GOE penalty.
              <Under-rotatedMore than a quarter but less than half a rotation short. Lower base value.
              <<DowngradedHalf a rotation or more short. Counted as a jump with one rotation less (4T<< is scored as 3T).
              eWrong edgeA Lutz or flip taken off from the wrong edge. Often seen on the Lutz.
              !Unclear edgeThe take-off edge isn't clear. Smaller penalty than e.
              FFallThe skater fell on the element: GOE −5 and a one-point deduction.
              +CombinationTwo or three jumps with no step between them: 4Lz+3T. In a combination, the extra jumps can only be toe loops or loops.
              +1Eu+EulerA small linking jump (half-loop) that connects a combination: 3F+1Eu+3S.

              Spins

              Three basic positions, plus variations. Levels 1–4 (or B for base) depend on difficult features: changes of foot, edge, position and speed.

              Upright spinUSp

              Standing on one leg. Variations: layback (LSp), back arched; Biellmann (Більман), free leg pulled over the head from behind.

              Sit spinSSp

              Spinning low, with the skating knee bent and the free leg stretched forward («вовчок»).

              Camel spinCSp

              Body and free leg parallel to the ice, like a letter T («ластівка»).

              Combination spinCoSp · CCoSp

              Changes of position. A C before the code means a change of foot. F means a flying entry: FCSp.

              Step & choreographic sequences

              Step sequenceStSq1–4

              Turns and steps (brackets, rockers, counters, twizzles) covering the whole rink. Levels 1–4.

              Choreographic sequenceChSq1

              A free, artistic passage in the free skate: spirals, spread eagles, hydroblades. Judged mostly by GOE.

              Spread eaglemove

              Gliding on both feet with toes pointing outwards and heels facing each other, in a straight line or curve.

              Hydroblademove

              A very deep lean, the body almost touching the ice, on a strong edge.

              Pairs elements

              Twist lift3Tw4

              The man throws the woman up; she rotates (here three times) in the air and is caught by the waist.

              Throw jump3LzTh · 4STh

              The man throws the woman into a jump; she lands alone on one foot.

              Lift5ALi4

              The woman is held above the man's head as he turns. Groups 1–5; group 5 (lasso, Axel take-off) is the hardest.

              Death spiralBoDs · FiDs

              «Тодес»: the man pivots while the woman circles around him, her head almost touching the ice.

              Side-by-side jumps3T+2T

              Both partners jump at the same time. They must be perfectly in time.

              Pair spinPCoSp4

              Spinning together on one axis, changing positions.

              Ice dance elements

              TwizzlesSyTw4

              «Твізли»: fast one-foot turns travelling across the ice, done by both partners in sync. Errors here cost the French champions points in Milan.

              Dance liftsStaLi · RoLi · CuLi

              Stationary, rotational, curve, straight-line or combination lifts. Shorter than pairs lifts, without lifting over the head.

              Pattern dancePSt · in RD

              A set step pattern danced to a given rhythm, chosen by the ISU each season.

              One-foot step sequenceOFTW

              Both partners skate difficult turns on one foot each, side by side.
